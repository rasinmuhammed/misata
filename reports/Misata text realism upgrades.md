# Rewire Misata's Text Before Adding Words

Misata's free text looks fake mainly because of how it is wired, not because its grammars are weak. In v0.9.7, text columns are routed by column-name heuristics to about 80 independent samplers. Most of those samplers are `rng.choice` over flat pools of 8–30 strings, and no row-level state is passed between columns. The result is **8 distinct product descriptions across 500 products, 120 distinct ticket subjects and descriptions across 2,000 tickets, 15 distinct resolution notes, and ticket subjects that agree with their own description in 7.0% of rows**, which is exactly what two independent draws over 15 issues would give. Reviews are the exception, and they show the fix. They use a deep recursive grammar with one entry symbol per star level, a post-pass that regenerates text from the final rating, and slots filled from row values. They reach 9,949 distinct values per 10k rows. The research on other tools points the same way. Every system with coherent text, from NVIDIA's Data Designer to PersonaHub and Cosmopedia, gets its diversity and coherence by **sampling conditioning attributes first and rendering text from them**. Misata can do that deterministically, with no LLM in the data path. The plan has four tiers. P0 is a week of routing and provenance fixes. P1 adds coherence post-passes and cross-table context. P2 adds per-row "scenario frames", properly licensed reference data and offline-built phrase banks. P3 adds a published benchmark against real data. Each tier should ship together with stricter text checks. Measured locally on seven real open corpora, those checks show Misata's current template text sitting 5–50× outside the real-data range.

**One correction to an earlier suspicion:** declared-choice categoricals are **not** flattened. In v0.9.7, declared `choices` without probabilities get Zipf–Mandelbrot weights and fail a uniformity test in every declaration form tested. The "balanced category" warnings came from text columns drawn uniformly from small pools, which the checker mistakes for categoricals.

A note on evidence. The egress proxy blocked arXiv, the ACL Anthology, most vendor documentation (Tonic, YData, Syntho, NVIDIA docs) and many licence pages (GeoNames, Hugging Face, O*NET, Yelp, Amazon Reviews). Paper findings and several licence statements therefore rest on search-result snippets and READMEs, not full reads. This report flags where that matters, most of all for licences, which must be re-verified on the primary page before any data ships. The codebase findings are firmer. Every one was checked by reading or running the code on branch `claude/claim-credits-0lzpbj` at v0.9.7. The evaluation thresholds are this session's own local measurements, not published numbers.

## Eight values per column: the root causes are routing and provenance

Text values reach a column through a priority chain in the simulator's TEXT branch: reference tables, capsule conditional vocabularies, user capsule vocabularies, patterns, `smart_generate` (off by default), then `_infer_semantic` feeding `RealisticTextGenerator`, and finally a legacy pool sampler ([simulator.py:2763-3036](../misata/simulator.py)). `_infer_semantic` is a roughly 300-line if-chain on column and table substrings ([realism.py:1011-1317](../misata/realism.py)). Columns are generated in declared order, so a generator can only see columns declared before it ([simulator.py:531-556](../misata/simulator.py)). Coherence is patched afterwards in `apply_realism_rules`, and only for city/country, email/name, category/product name and review/rating ([realism.py:2337-2403](../misata/realism.py)). Every symptom reproduced on a 4-table, 6,500-row dict schema except categorical flattening. Each traces to a specific line.

Two patterns run through this table. First, **built-in defaults are placed in the domain capsule as if they were data**, and `DomainCapsule.get_values` returns them ahead of better per-category or grammar fallbacks ([domain_capsule.py:54-59](../misata/domain_capsule.py)). The provenance tag `misata-defaults` should mean "use only when nothing better exists", but only two call sites honour it ([simulator.py:527](../misata/simulator.py), [realism.py:483-490](../misata/realism.py)). Second, **coherence is impossible by construction** when subject, description, resolution and priority are separate draws. Description wording such as "urgent" appears in 9–11% of rows at every priority. Mean price is $56–68 in every category, so the category-blind price defaults found in the earlier v0.9.6.59 audit are still present in v0.9.7.

Capacity numbers show why adding synonyms will not help on its own. Enumerating every reachable string gives 78,955,835 derivations for `review_3` and 6,253 for the notes grammar, which saturates completely by 100k rows. Ticket bodies without context reach 1,050, and the comment grammar 115. Every flat pool in [vocab_seeds.py](../misata/vocab_seeds.py) is tiny: TICKET_SUBJECTS 25, RESOLUTION_NOTES 15, CLINICAL_NOTES 8, CUSTOMER_FEEDBACK 12, and 3–5 product descriptions per category. A reader notices **top-value share (4–13%) and repeated openers** before they notice raw distinct counts, so they see the same sentence about every 8–25 rows. Opening the existing Lexicon path matters as much as growing pools. Lexicon is reached only when a semantic is declared, and there company_name reaches 6,720/10k against 28 values on the undeclared path ([lexicon.py:326-450](../misata/lexicon.py)).

On the categorical question, the categorical branch applies weights `w_k ∝ (k+q)^-s` with s=0.85 and q=2, using a CRC32-seeded per-column rank permutation when `sampling="auto"` ([simulator.py:2053,2071-2095](../misata/simulator.py)). Across `string+choices`, `string+enum`, `categorical+choices` and columns named priority, status, tier and category, every column showed max shares of 0.28–0.42 and chi² p≈0 against uniform. The checker `_check_category_balance` runs on any column with 3–30 levels and at least 300 rows ([tells.py:260-279](../misata/tells.py)), so it flags 8–28-value *text* pools as balanced categories. The one real distortion of a declared category is the product-name rewrite pass. The Zipf exponent is also mild, giving only about a 1.8× max/min ratio for four choices. That ratio is detectable at n=2,000 but still looks nearly balanced to the eye.

Performance is not a constraint on fixing any of this. The full repro ran in 2.23 s. Reviews cost about 1.2 s per 10k rows and are **generated twice**: once at column time and again by `_fix_review_sentiment`, which throws the first result away ([realism.py:2444-2488](../misata/realism.py)). Per-row Python `rng.choice` loops in [microtext.py:572-679](../misata/microtext.py) are the other hot spot. Vectorised semantics take 1 ms or less per 10k rows.

## The field converged on "sample attributes, then render text"

Nobody in the field enforces text coherence with constraints. Every tool with coherent text gets it from generation order. Faker and Mimesis have no coherence mechanism at all. Faker's lorem joins random words ("Agent every development say.") and `catch_phrase` takes one word from each of three tuples ([Faker company provider](https://github.com/joke2k/faker/blob/master/faker/providers/company/__init__.py)). Mimesis `text()` draws from **28 fixed English sentences, mostly about programming languages** ([mimesis text.json](https://github.com/lk-geimfari/mimesis/blob/master/mimesis/datasets/en/text.json)). Community providers only add word lists. faker-commerce produced "Wooden Pizza" in category "Sports" in a local run, and its PyPI blurb promises descriptions that the installed v1.0.4 does not provide ([PyPI faker-commerce](https://pypi.org/project/faker-commerce/)). Misata's slot-filling grammars are already ahead of both.

**NVIDIA NeMo Data Designer** (Apache-2.0, the open successor to Gretel Navigator) is the best model to copy. It is a declarative DAG in which cheap sampler columns, such as category, a `subcategory` sampler keyed by parent value, persona and weighted `review_style`, are explicitly described as "steering the diversity" of LLM text columns. Those columns then reference the samplers through Jinja: "…recently purchased a product called {{ product_name }}… gave a rating of {{ number_of_stars }} stars. The style… should be '{{ review_style }}'" ([Data Designer tutorial](https://github.com/NVIDIA-NeMo/DataDesigner/blob/main/docs/notebook_source/1-the-basics.py)). Its validator emits `PROMPT_WITHOUT_REFERENCES` when a prompt references no columns, because "the same prompt will be used for every row" ([validation.py](https://github.com/NVIDIA-NeMo/DataDesigner/blob/main/packages/data-designer-engine/src/data_designer/engine/validation.py)). That lint translates directly to Misata as "this template references no row columns". The LLM pipelines show the same pattern. DataDreamer's AttrPrompt fills attribute grids into instructions ([DataDreamer](https://github.com/datadreamer-dev/DataDreamer/blob/main/src/steps/prompt/data_from_attributed_prompt.py)). PersonaHub prepends a persona to every task ([persona-hub templates](https://github.com/tencent-ailab/persona-hub/blob/main/code/prompt_templates.py)). Cosmopedia conditions on audience and style, and its MinHash dedup found **under 1% duplicates** across 30M files, so dedup was cleanup, not the source of diversity ([Cosmopedia dedup](https://github.com/huggingface/cosmopedia/blob/main/deduplication/README.md)).

The commercial field has consolidated around two other patterns, neither of which fits Misata's no-source, deterministic niche. **Learned joint models**, such as Mostly AI's `LANGUAGE_TEXT` encoding, fit a from-scratch LSTM or LoRA-fine-tuned causal LM conditioned on the synthesized tabular columns. They need source data, and "a modern GPU is highly recommended" ([mostlyai README](https://github.com/mostly-ai/mostlyai/blob/main/README.md); [arXiv 2508.00718](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.00718v1), snippet). The company behind MOSTLY AI ceased operations in March 2026 and its brand went to Syntho ([beri.net](https://www.beri.net/article/best-synthetic-data-platforms-real-data-cannot-leave-2026); Syntho announcement via snippet only). **Prompt-time agents with validator loops** include Tonic Fabricate's data agent paired with a Validation Agent "in a loop" ([Tonic press release](https://www.tonic.ai/press-releases/tonic-launches-fabricate-data-agent), snippet) and YData's text-to-dataset synthesizer. YData's `calculated_features` (compute deterministically anything that has one correct answer) matches Misata's own philosophy ([YData docs](https://docs.sdk.ydata.ai/latest/synthetic_data/text_to_dataset/), snippet).

LLMs in the per-row path carry two costs that conflict with Misata's promises. The first is **determinism**. llama.cpp does not guarantee bit-identical output when batch size or KV-cache sharing varies. Its batch-invariant mode targets CUDA ([llama.cpp PR #16016](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/16016)), and reproducibility advice reduces to temperature 0, which removes diversity ([llama-cpp-python #972](https://github.com/abetlen/llama-cpp-python/issues/972)). Data Designer fingerprints configs, not outputs ([fingerprint.py](https://github.com/NVIDIA-NeMo/DataDesigner/blob/main/packages/data-designer-config/src/data_designer/config/fingerprint.py)). The second is **throughput**. A 135M model on CPU reportedly manages 20–100 tok/s ([45Squared](https://45squared.com/llms-that-run-on-cpu-only-hardware/), a secondary source). That works out to roughly 1.2 s per 60-token review, against microseconds for grammars. Hosted APIs are cheap in dollars, an estimated $0.28 per 1k rows for one review column at GPT-5-mini rates ([inworld.ai pricing](https://inworld.ai/models/openai-gpt-5-mini)). Latency and reproducibility are the binding constraints, not price. LLM output also collapses on its own. In 20,000 sampled stories, "Elias" appeared in 26.5% ([Hamilton & Mimno, arXiv 2605.26492](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.26492)). "Kenji Nakamura" made up 40.3% of one persona study's samples ([Springer AIR 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10462-026-11641-3)). Verbalized sampling recovers 1.6–2.1× diversity ([arXiv 2510.01171](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.01171v3)). For Misata, the conclusion is to use LLMs only **offline, to build versioned phrase banks over explicit attribute grids**. Names should never come from an LLM. They should come from frequency tables. This research found no open tool shipping attribute-keyed, LLM-pre-generated phrase banks as a feature, which suggests an open niche, though that rests on absence of evidence.

## A deterministic engine can borrow almost the whole design

The structural fix is to generalise something Misata already has. `_person_frame` makes one joint person draw per table, shared by first name, last name, username and email ([realism.py:1673-1685](../misata/realism.py)). That is already a latent row frame. A `_scenario_frame` beside it would draw one scenario per row from a catalogue, and each text column would render that scenario instead of sampling independently. A **ticket scenario** would hold `{issue_key, category, product_area, symptom, error_detail, steps, root_cause, fix_action, priority_dist}`. From it, the subject becomes a short noun phrase ("Checkout payment declined"), the description becomes the existing `ticket_bodies` grammar seeded with the same symptom, priority is drawn from the scenario's distribution, and resolution notes render root cause plus fix, null unless the status is resolved or closed. A **product scenario** would hold `{category, subcategory, base_noun, attributes, price_band}`. The name, the description grammar and a log-uniform price inside the capsule's existing price bands ([simulator.py:432](../misata/simulator.py)) all render from it. Category is never rewritten afterwards, because category caused the draw, which retires `_fix_category_from_product_name` for generated rows. When category is declared, the scenario draw conditions on it rather than overriding it. A post-pass form modelled on `_fix_review_sentiment` keeps the result independent of column order and lets protected, user-declared columns win. A **persona** per entity, seeded from `hash(seed, entity_id)`, adds register, verbosity, typo rate and emoji propensity. A customer's bio, reviews and tickets then share a voice regardless of row order. Child tables also need **FK-joined prose context**. Today 24.5% of reviews say "the unit" or "the item", because the reviews table has only `product_id` and `_prose_context` never looks across the join ([realism.py:2421-2441](../misata/realism.py)).

Inside each column, the template "rhythm" a careful reader spots comes from a fixed sentence count, repeated openers and repeated syntactic skeletons. Shaib et al. found length-6 POS templates in **95% of model outputs vs 38% of human references** on Rotten Tomatoes ([EMNLP 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.368/), via snippet). The fixes are structural. **Plan then realize**: sample a discourse plan per row (core, optional context, optional attempted fix, ask), with a sentence count from a fitted distribution and a target length drawn from a log-normal. Then expand each move from its own sub-grammar. Add **opener quotas** within a batch, optional discourse markers and clause reordering, and a seeded **register and noise layer** (lowercase starts, missing punctuation, contractions, low-rate typos) keyed to persona. Add **open vocabulary** from row entities and large category lexicons, so vocabulary grows with N. The microtext docstring already admits that fixed pools keep the Heaps exponent at zero. Tracery-style modifiers handle English agreement cheaply ([Tracery paper](http://www.galaxykate.com/pdfs/ComptonKybartasMateas15-Tracery%20An%20Author-Focused%20Generative%20Text%20Tool.pdf)).

Two further sources of human texture are worth having, each with conditions. **Offline phrase banks** should be built per (domain, column, sentiment or severity, persona facet) cell. The build would prompt with verbalized sampling across two or three model families, segment the output into slot-tagged clauses, dedup with MinHash and embeddings, strip names, and ship versioned compressed JSON of a few MB per domain. This gives near-LLM texture with byte-identical runtime output. The licence terms for LLM output (Llama, Qwen and Gemma model-output clauses) were not researched and must be checked first. **Order-2 Markov chains** are cheap and MIT-friendly through markovify, which rejects outputs that overlap the source by 15 words or 70% ([markovify](https://github.com/jsvine/markovify)). They memorise small corpora, though, and markovify uses the global `random` module. They belong as a phrase-level slot filler compiled to CSR arrays for vectorised `searchsorted` sampling, trained only on permissively licensed, PII-free text, and filtered against source k-grams.

## Real text falls in a narrow band that Misata's templates miss by 5–50×

Mainstream tabular benchmarks barely evaluate text. SDMetrics "mostly focuses on unimodal data types", and text metrics such as BERTScore and MAUVE "do not test row-level cross-modal consistency" ([arXiv 2609.22149](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.22149), snippet). Misata's single `text_templates` tell fires only on columns with ≥200 rows, mean length ≥40 characters and a space in every value, and only on exact duplicates above 20% or a top three-word opener above 10%. That gate misses short ticket subjects, and the opener rule missed `customer_feedback` (0.098) and `ticket_subject` (0.049) even though 99% of their rows are duplicates. Shaib et al. recommend gzip **compression ratio** as the first-line diversity score, because it captures "all token/type ratio related alternatives" at almost no cost ([ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.ijcnlp-demo.5/); [cshaib/diversity](https://github.com/cshaib/diversity)).

To set thresholds, this research ran a **local calibration** at a fixed n=2,000 values per corpus. The corpora were Women's Clothing E-Commerce Reviews ([mirror](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/AFAgarap/ecommerce-reviews-analysis/master/Womens%20Clothing%20E-Commerce%20Reviews.csv)), AG News descriptions ([mirror](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mhjabreel/CharCnn_Keras/master/data/ag_news_csv/test.csv)), TweetEval ([cardiffnlp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cardiffnlp/tweeteval/main/datasets/sentiment/test_text.txt)), StackOverflow posts as a ticket stand-in ([kavgan](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/kavgan/nlp-in-practice/master/tf-idf/data/stackoverflow-data-idf.json)) and Goodbooks titles ([zygmuntz](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/zygmuntz/goodbooks-10k/master/books.csv)). These are this session's measurements on one sample each, not published norms.

Every real long-text corpus sat at gzip CR ≤2.95, distinct-3 ≥0.71, near-duplicates ≤0.2% and length CV ≥0.29. Template text sat at CR 15–103 and distinct-3 ≤0.015. Faker lorem is the trap. It passes every n-gram diversity metric, but its Zipf slope of -0.14 and its vocabulary of 971 types expose it as word salad. Short title-like columns legitimately repeat: real review titles have 13.6% exact duplicates and 63% skeleton duplicates. Skeleton and near-duplicate checks therefore need a median-length gate of 8 or more tokens. gzip CR is nearly size-invariant above about 500 rows. Distinct-n is not (distinct-2 fell from 0.62 to 0.35 between n=200 and 2,000), so it must be computed on a fixed sample. MTLD failed to separate real from template text (Misata clinical notes scored 112, inside the real range of 57–355), and Self-BLEU is O(n²) and correlated with CR, so neither is worth adding.

Cross-column agreement tests catch the most common template-generator tell, which is text drawn independently of its row. They run on CPU with no heavy dependencies. On real reviews (local calibration), VADER sentiment vs star rating gave **Spearman 0.436** and rose monotonically from 0.24 at 1★ to 0.87 at 5★ ([vaderSentiment](https://pypi.org/project/vaderSentiment/)). The share of title words found in the body was 0.403 for real pairs against 0.097 shuffled. A naive Bayes classifier predicting department from text reached 0.761 accuracy against a 0.438 majority baseline, and fell below baseline when labels were shuffled. Misata's own ticket subject/description agreement (7.0%) and description/name overlap (1%) are what the shuffled baselines would predict. Reference-based measures (MAUVE, [arXiv 2102.01454](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.01454); TF-IDF classifier two-sample AUC) need an embedding model or scikit-learn and belong in an optional extra. LLM judges favour low-perplexity, familiar text ([arXiv 2410.21819](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.21819)), so they can only be an advisory signal: blinded, order-randomised and from a different model family than the generator. At dataset level, the Synthea-style blinded Turing test is cheap to document: 20 real and 20 synthetic rows, three or more raters, accuracy with a binomial CI ([BMC Med Inform 2019](https://bmcmedinformdecismak.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12911-019-0793-0)). One consequence has to be planned for. The stricter checks will flag Misata's own default output, where `enrich_text` returned 4–12 unique strings per 2,000 rows. The checks and the generator fixes therefore have to ship together.

## Conclusion

The headline realism claim is closer than the symptoms suggest. Misata already contains every mechanism it needs: a review grammar with 10^5–10^7 derivations per star level, a latent per-row person frame, an order-independent regeneration post-pass and a Lexicon engine with thousands of names. What it lacks is plumbing that lets those mechanisms reach the columns users actually declare. A week of P0 work removes most of the visible tells. Most of that work is deleting precedence mistakes, and very little is new content. The deeper insight from the field is that **coherence and diversity are the same problem**. Both come from sampling the row's latent story first. Misata can do this without an LLM, unlike Data Designer, so it keeps its byte-identical guarantee.

The measurement work matters as much as the generator work, and there is a strategic opening in it. No tabular benchmark tests whether text agrees with its own row. The local calibration shows that a handful of stdlib checks separate real text from template text by one to two orders of magnitude. If Misata ships those checks, publishes their thresholds and passes them on its own defaults, it defines what "realistic synthetic text" means for no-source tools. The remaining uncertainties are a reason to measure rather than assert. The thresholds come from seven English corpora, the licences were read through snippets, and LLM-written text was never calibrated.
