# Misata Wins by Guaranteeing What Models Cannot

Misata becomes the definitive synthetic data library by owning the category that learned models structurally cannot enter. That category is **coherent, constraint-exact, multi-table data with no real data required, byte-reproducible across versions and machines, and proven on a public benchmark**. It does not get there by chasing TabDiff or ClavaDDPM on their own fidelity leaderboards. The landscape research shows that every learned state-of-the-art generator needs training data. The best relational models still cannot handle common schema shapes: REaLTabFormer handles only linear chains, and ClavaDDPM cannot model two foreign keys between the same pair of tables. They also fail on behavioural and temporal patterns. Meanwhile the open developer-seeding slot has emptied. Snaplet shut down, Neosync was archived, Gretel's product went into NVIDIA, MOSTLY AI's brand went to Syntho, and SDV is source-available under BUSL with its scalable multi-table synthesizers paywalled. The next release should therefore be **1.0, built around three proofs Misata can make and no competitor can**:

1. **A cross-version, cross-platform determinism contract.** A counter-based RNG keyed per (table, column, row block), with Misata's own distribution transforms and golden-hash CI. 1.0 is the last moment to break the RNG stream once, and then never again.
2. **Free text that passes its own gzip and diversity gate on every text kind.** This closes the eight kinds still at 4.0–4.9x with a skeleton-plus-infill engine and licence-clean phrase banks, without an LLM in the data path.
3. **A public, five-track benchmark** that runs real-vs-real as the ceiling and fitted models as baselines.

Two hygiene items belong in the same release. The first is removing the BUSL-licensed SDV dependency from the copula path. The second is withdrawing the claim that `mimic` produces "privacy-safe" twins, which is a non-differentially-private claim the literature says cannot be backed. Differentially private fit-to-sample (AIM/MST via Private-PGM), calibrate-from-published-aggregates, a world-wide priors pack and distributed sharding follow in 1.x. The single most important thing to say candidly is that Misata should never claim to match a specific real dataset's joint distribution as well as models trained on it. Misata's claim is different and more defensible: with zero access to the data, it lands within a measured distance of tools that trained on it, while guaranteeing what they cannot.

A note on evidence. The research sessions' egress proxy blocked arXiv full text, OpenReview, Hugging Face, the SDV docs, pypistats and Hacker News comments. Many paper numbers therefore rest on abstracts and search-result extracts of the cited primary sources. Licence statements in particular must be re-verified on the live page before any data ships. Throughput figures labelled "local probe" come from a script run in this session on a 4-vCPU Xeon at 2.8 GHz with NumPy 2.4.6. Statements about Misata's own code were checked against the v0.9.7 checkout.

## Learned models need data and break on real schemas, leaving the no-data slot open

Single-table research has moved from GANs to diffusion and fast autoregressive models. TabSyn (ICLR 2024) reports cutting column-distribution error by **86%** and pairwise-correlation error by **67%** against the strongest baselines ([TabSyn](https://cdn.amazon.science/a8/d6/054330464506b2b3bb2566db838f/mixed-type-tabular-data-synthesis-with-score-based-diffusion-in-latent-space.pdf)). TabDiff (ICLR 2025) beats TabSyn by a further **22.6%** on pairwise trends and **15.0%** on downstream ML efficacy ([TabDiff](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/5c882988ce5fac487974ee4f415b96a9-Paper-Conference.pdf)). MOSTLY AI's TabularARGN reaches near-TabSyn fidelity on Adult (97.9% vs 98.2% accuracy) in **138 s against 2,316 s**, roughly 16x faster ([TabularARGN](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.12012)). On the relational side, ClavaDDPM (NeurIPS 2024) beats the best baseline by **58.29% on 2-hop correlations** (Instacart) and **20.24% on 3-hop** (Berka). It is only "competitive" on single-column densities and cardinality ([ClavaDDPM](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/983876577ec81db17ecfae1521df9208-Paper-Conference.pdf)). RelDiff (2025) improves k-hop correlation preservation by a further **25.3%** on average ([RelDiff](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.00710)). These are real gains, and on their home turf of reproducing a given dataset's joint distribution they will keep winning. Misata should not contest that.

The same literature documents ceilings that a schema-first engine does not have. **REaLTabFormer can only generate linear parent-to-child chains, and ClavaDDPM cannot model two or more foreign keys between the same pair of tables** ([RelDiff](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.00710v1)). That rules out an ordinary `transfers(from_account, to_account)` table. In the Hudovernik relational benchmark, an XGBoost discriminator told REaLTabFormer's AirBnB output from real data with accuracy **≈1.0**, and its ML utility was "near naive baseline" ([arXiv 2410.03411](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.03411v1)). A 2026 fraud benchmark finds that learned generators **fail to preserve temporal, velocity and multi-account signals** ([arXiv 2604.13125](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.13125v1)). Seq2Synth finds that "strong static-distribution fidelity does not reliably translate to temporal fidelity", and near-perfect static models still emit duplicate timestamps and irregular intervals ([Seq2Synth](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15606)). Learned generators also carry privacy risk that no-data generation does not. LLM-based tabular generators reach mean worst-case membership-inference AUCs up to **0.667** (LLaMA 3.3 70B), against 0.580–0.627 for CTGAN, TVAE and TabDiff ([Risk in Context](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.17066)), and they can regurgitate training strings ([When Tables Leak](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.08875)). A telling detail: the TabPFN tabular foundation models are pretrained entirely on tables drawn from hand-designed structural-causal-model priors ([TabPFN-2.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.08667)). The best tabular models in the world are trained on principled prior-based synthetic data, which is Misata's paradigm.

The user complaints line up with that gap. SDV's HMA "may sample foreign key values that were not present in the original data" ([SDV #1581](https://github.com/sdv-dev/SDV/issues/1581)) and "always creates a child for every parent row" ([#1673](https://github.com/sdv-dev/SDV/issues/1673)). It errors on multi-parent references and constraints ([#2136](https://github.com/sdv-dev/SDV/issues/2136), [#2087](https://github.com/sdv-dev/SDV/issues/2087)). One user reported **about 3 days for 1M rows with constraints** ([SDV #2110](https://github.com/sdv-dev/SDV/issues/2110)). SDV is BUSL-1.1, which forbids offering a commercial "Synthetic Data Service" ([SDV LICENSE](https://github.com/sdv-dev/SDV/blob/main/LICENSE)). Its HSA and Independent multi-table synthesizers are Enterprise-only ([DataCebo](https://datacebo.com/blog/multi-table-synthesizers/)), and Enterprise starts at **$500/month plus usage** ([DataCebo pricing](https://datacebo.com/pricing/)). SDV 2.0 (September 2026) still learns "from a representative subset" of real data ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/datacebo-releases-sdv-2-0-for-building-generative-relational-models-of-enterprise-data-302879009.html)).

The strategic reading is clear. Every surviving commercial leader needs real data, either to train on (SDV, Syntho, YData, NVIDIA Safe Synthesizer) or to transform (Tonic). Data Designer, the closest no-data rival, is LLM-bound and gives up byte-reproducibility. **Misata's defensible headline is "the best you can do without data, with guarantees no learned model gives"**:

- zero orphans on arbitrary FK graphs, including multi-FK, diamond and self-referencing schemas;
- exact declared aggregates;
- correct temporal and behavioural structure;
- zero memorisation risk;
- a permissive licence.

The constraint guarantee is the same one SDV sells as paid CAG, marketed as valid "100% of the time" ([CAG](https://datacebo.com/announcements/introducing-cag/)). Every one of those claims is testable, so the benchmark below is what turns them from marketing into evidence.

## A five-track benchmark turns "realistic" into a falsifiable claim

Misata already has the seed of this benchmark, and it is more favourable than expected. On Olist (30,000 orders), `misata_schema`, which never sees the data, reaches a detection AUC of **0.738**. SDV's Gaussian copula, fitted on 15,000 real orders, scores **0.709**. `misata_schema` also gets product fan-out almost exactly right (ΔGini 0.028). `misata_mimic` reaches **0.541**, close to the 0.498 real-vs-real floor ([docs/realism-benchmark.md](../docs/realism-benchmark.md)). The same page is admirably candid about two weaknesses. Taxi fares are still wrong (AUC 0.911). And the e-commerce amount prior "was set from Olist's public data", so that row is not a blind result. The benchmark's design should now be hardened so that outsiders trust it, following the two community models that work. NIST's Collaborative Research Cycle has collected **over 350 deidentified instances** of fixed public data. Each one is scored with a standardized battery and archived in human- and machine-readable form ([NIST CRC](https://pages.nist.gov/privacy_collaborative_research_cycle/pages/citing.html)). SyntheRela runs a maintainer-run leaderboard on fixed hardware with rate-limited submissions, and is installable with `pip install syntherela` ([SyntheRela](https://github.com/martinjurkovic/syntherela)).

The metric choice matters, because the cheap metrics saturate. An ICML 2025 position paper found that the precision/recall, density/coverage and α-precision/β-recall families "fail a large number of sanity checks, in many cases failing to measure even the basic property they are supposed to measure" ([arXiv 2505.22450](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22450)). Classifier detection keeps its power. Hudovernik et al. found that "no method is able to synthesize a dataset that is indistinguishable from original data" ([arXiv 2410.03411](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03411)). The toughest published multi-table test is SyntheRela's aggregation-enriched detection, which adds child counts and means to parent rows before training the discriminator ([SyntheRela](https://github.com/martinjurkovic/syntherela)). For text, gzip compression ratio captures what slower n-gram homogeneity scores capture ([Shaib et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.00553)).

| Track | What it measures | Reference needed | Why Misata should lead or lose honestly |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Validity | PK uniqueness, zero orphans ([SDMetrics ReferentialIntegrity](https://docs.sdv.dev/sdmetrics/metrics/metrics-glossary/referentialintegrity)), CHECK, width and range adherence, temporal order, on deliberately hard schemas (multi-FK, diamonds, self-refs, circular FKs) | None | Schema-first should hit 100%; publish the SDV #1581/#1673/#2136 cases next to it |
| 2. Reference-free realism | Misata's 14 tells and text checks, run on every contestant and on real data as calibration | None | Real data must score 1.0, or the tells are wrong |
| 3. Held-out fidelity | KS/TV complements, pair trends, NIST-style 3-way k-marginals ([arXiv 2306.13216](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.13216)), cardinality shape, ClavaDDPM k-hop correlation, SyntheRela aggregation detection, against a real holdout | Public DB, split | Misata competes in "zero-shot" and "prior + published aggregates" categories; fitted models train on the train half |
| 4. Workload | A fixed SQL/BI workload (group-bys, top-k, FK-path joins, time buckets) with per-query relative error; TSTR on one or two tasks for academic comparability | Public DB | Fidelity and utility rank methods differently ([arXiv 2410.03411](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03411)) |
| 5. Temporal and human | Seq2Synth's timestamp, cross-sectional and longitudinal dimensions; a blinded rater study (20 real + 20 synthetic rows, 3+ raters, binomial CI) | Public DB, raters | Learned models are weakest here ([Seq2Synth](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15606)) |

Five design rules make the results credible. First, report real-vs-real (train vs holdout) as the ceiling on every metric. Second, publish per-property breakdowns rather than one aggregate score. Third, pin datasets with checksums and emit SDNist-style JSON plus HTML per run. Fourth, include Faker-style scripts, SDV HMA and ClavaDDPM as baselines. Fifth, add new datasets that were never used to tune Misata's priors, so "blind" means blind. The licence issue sharpens the last rule. Olist is **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0** ([Kaggle Olist](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/olistbr/brazilian-ecommerce)), and Misata ships an e-commerce amount prior derived from it ([domain_priors.py](../misata/domain_priors.py)). Whether fitted parameters from NC data count as "adapted material" is legally unresolved. A conservative MIT package should not rest on that argument. The prior should be re-derived from **Online Retail II (CC BY 4.0, 1,067,371 transactions)** ([UCI](https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/502/online+retail+ii)) or BLS Consumer Expenditure data (public domain) ([BLS CE](https://www.bls.gov/cex/pumd_data.htm)). Olist can stay as an evaluation dataset that the harness downloads rather than redistributes. That is an inference about NC terms, not legal advice. The benchmark's LLM-discriminator component, if kept, must use several model families, because detection rates swung from 0% to 100% across models on the same synthesizers in one 2026 study ([arXiv 2606.09865](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09865)).

## Closing the last eight text kinds needs skeleton-plus-infill, not bigger pools

Misata's text work since the last report has paid off. Reviews, ticket text, notes, feedback, posts, captions, email subjects and bios now pass the gzip, trigram, skeleton and near-copy checks at 2,000 rows. Eight kinds remain above the 4x warning line ([LIMITATIONS.md](../LIMITATIONS.md)):

- churn, cancellation and return reasons: 4.0–4.3x;
- clinical notes: about 4.1x;
- product descriptions: 4.3–4.5x;
- delivery instructions: about 4.7x;
- chief complaints: about 4.8x;
- discharge instructions: about 4.9x, with 34% distinct trigrams.

The mechanism tells you where to push. gzip's window is 32 KB, so its ratio mostly measures how often 3+ byte substrings recur within about 32 KB of text. For English text it typically lands at roughly 2.5–3.3x ([GNU gzip](http://www.gnu.org/s/gzip/manual/gzip.html)). Moving a grammar from 4.5x to under 3x means removing **long verbatim spans shared across rows**: fixed skeletons, a small connective set and closed slot pools. Adding synonyms does little. The data-to-text literature agrees that templates guarantee content fidelity but "often suffer from low diversity" ([Jagfeld et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.04864)). No published study gives a phrase-bank size that yields a target diversity, so the targets have to be set empirically, against the gate Misata already runs.

The highest-leverage technique is a **template skeleton with short n-gram infill**. Fixed anchors carry the row's facts (the product, the complaint, the drug). Between them, a slot-conditioned Kneser-Ney 3- or 4-gram model samples 3–12 tokens and stops on a sentence boundary. N-gram sampling is fluent over about four-word windows and drifts beyond that ([KenLM](https://www.kheafield.com/papers/avenue/kenlm.pdf)), which is exactly the window an infill span needs. KenLM is LGPL-2.1 ([kenlm](https://kheafield.com/code/kenlm/)), so use it, or NLTK, only at build time. Misata would ship pruned count tables as NumPy arrays and sample them with its own seeded, vectorised code. The licence that matters is then the training corpus's, not KenLM's. The candidates were checked against that requirement:

- **FineWeb** is ODC-By plus Common Crawl terms ([FineWeb](https://huggingface.co/datasets/HuggingFaceFW/fineweb)). It is redistributable with a NOTICE when filtered to review- and support-like pages.
- **Synthea** and its clinical-notes sibling **chatty-notes** are Apache-2.0 ([Synthea](https://github.com/synthetichealth/synthea/blob/master/LICENSE); [chatty-notes](https://github.com/synthetichealth/chatty-notes)). They are the only clean clinical source found.
- **Yelp** is academic or non-commercial only ([Yelp terms](https://s3-media0.fl.yelpcdn.com/assets/srv0/engineering_pages/f64cb2d3efcc/assets/vendor/Dataset_User_Agreement.pdf)), and **Amazon Reviews 2023** has no grantable licence ([McAuley Lab](https://huggingface.co/datasets/McAuley-Lab/Amazon-Reviews-2023/discussions/1)). Both are out, even for tuning statistics.
- **Wikipedia-derived** text carries CC BY-SA share-alike ([Wiki-40B](https://aclanthology.org/2020.lrec-1.297.pdf)). It belongs, if anywhere, in a separately licensed optional data package.

The second technique is **offline pre-generated phrase banks**, shipped as slot-keyed clause fragments rather than whole rows, so that recombination multiplies diversity. API provider terms assign output ownership to the customer and bar using outputs to train competing models ([OpenAI terms](https://openai.com/policies/row-terms-of-use/); [Anthropic Commercial Terms](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/commercial-terms?facet2=pdf)). Shipping text as data looks permissible, but it is unsettled. The cleaner path is an Apache-2.0 open-weight model such as Qwen2.5-0.5B ([Qwen](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct-GGUF)) or SmolLM2, run once at build time. The build should go through a fixed pipeline: exact dedup, MinHash 5-gram near-dedup, a scrub of real names and brands, per-slot length and sentiment balancing, and a per-field gzip and distinct-n gate before a bank version is frozen. A local LLM *in the data path* should stay an opt-in extra at most, for two reasons. The first is throughput. SmolLM2-135M runs at about **75 tokens/s** on an M2 MacBook ([llmapi](https://llmapi.ai/models/huggingfacetb-smollm2-135m/)), roughly 2–3 short rows per second per core, three orders of magnitude below Misata's text throughput. The second is determinism. llama.cpp output varies with slot count and KV-cache layout ([llama.cpp #7052](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/issues/7052)), and the root cause, floating-point non-associativity, survives quantisation ([arXiv 2506.09501](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09501v2)). Its contract could only be "same seed, same binary, same CPU path".

There are two candid caveats. First, the under-3x target was calibrated on reviews, news and StackOverflow posts. **No licence-clean corpus of real chief complaints, discharge instructions or delivery notes exists to calibrate against.** MIMIC is credentialed and non-redistributable. Real chief complaints are themselves formulaic ("chest pain x2 days"), so a 3x target for them may be wrong in both directions. Misata should report clinical and instruction text against a separately labelled "no real reference available" threshold rather than claim parity. Second, passing gzip does not defeat a word-n-gram classifier, and LIMITATIONS.md already says so. The accurate public claim is "passes reference-free diversity and row-agreement checks", not "undetectable".

## Licence-tiered priors fix the US flavour without importing legal risk

Misata's economic priors centre on US dollar magnitudes and US retail conventions, and its locale packs change formats rather than distributions ([LIMITATIONS.md](../LIMITATIONS.md)). The open-data landscape supports a world-wide priors pack, provided licences are handled as a first-class engineering artefact. Misata already has `misata/data_packs/SOURCES.toml` as the place to do it. The permissive tier is broad:

- **ILOSTAT** wages by occupation and country: CC BY 4.0 ([re3data](https://www.re3data.org/repository/r3d100013044), a secondary source).
- **OECD**: CC BY 4.0 for content published since July 2024 ([OECD T&C](https://www.oecd.org/en/about/terms-conditions.html)).
- **World Bank**: CC BY 4.0 with a prescribed attribution and no implied endorsement ([World Bank](https://www.worldbank.org/ext/en/legal/terms-conditions/datasets)).
- **Eurostat**: reuse for commercial purposes "provided the source is acknowledged" ([Eurostat](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/help/copyright-notice)).
- **US BLS**: public domain ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/bls/linksite.htm)).
- **UK ONS ASHE** percentiles by occupation and region: Open Government Licence ([ONS ASHE](https://www.ons.gov.uk/employmentandlabourmarket/peopleinwork/earningsandworkinghours/bulletins/annualsurveyofhoursandearnings/2025)).
- **Japan e-Stat**: commercial use allowed, CC BY 4.0-compatible ([e-Stat](https://www.e-stat.go.jp/en/terms-of-use)).
- **India GODL**: permits derivative commercial products ([GODL-India](https://smartcities.data.gov.in/government-open-data-license-india)).

Payment mixes are published as headline facts. Cash was **52% of euro-area point-of-sale transactions by number in 2024**, while cards were 45% and cash 39% by value ([ECB SPACE](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/stats/ecb_surveys/space/html/ecb.space2024~19d46f0f17.en.html)). In the US, cash was **14% of payments by number, credit cards 35% and debit cards 30%** ([Fed DCPC 2025](https://www.frbservices.org/news/research/2025-findings-from-the-diary-of-consumer-payment-choice)). That one contrast is a realism tell that today's US-default payment column gets wrong for every European dataset.

The design implication is that priors are data with provenance, not constants in code. Each file should ship fitted parameters or quantile vectors, never raw tables, with source, SPDX ID, URL, retrieval date and transformation recorded. CC BY's "indicate changes" clause requires this. Share-alike or ODbL derivatives belong in a separate optional package so the core wheel stays permissive. Where no open source exists, the prior should be labelled an assumption rather than data. This applies to churn rates, conversion rates, session timing and ticket volumes, where the research found nothing licence-clean. Distilled statistics are largely facts, but the EU sui generis database right still argues for relying on explicit licences rather than a "facts are free" argument.

## Fit-to-sample should be a residual model under schema rules, with DP as a separate claim

Misata is not starting from zero here. `mimic()` already profiles columns and detects pairwise Pearson correlations above a threshold ([profiler.py](../misata/profiler.py)). On Olist it scored better than SDV on most benchmark metrics. Two things are wrong with the current state, though. The optional `CopulaGenerator` wraps SDV's `GaussianCopulaSynthesizer` through the `[advanced]` extra ([copula.py](../misata/generators/copula.py); [pyproject.toml](../pyproject.toml)). `sdv` 1.38.5 and `copulas` 0.14.1 are both **BUSL-1.1** ([PyPI sdv](https://pypi.org/project/sdv/); [PyPI copulas](https://pypi.org/project/copulas/)), so an MIT library is quietly steering users into a source-available licence. The fix is an in-house Gaussian copula of roughly 150 lines of NumPy and SciPy: empirical CDF, normal scores, correlation matrix, and sampling through Misata's own Cholesky decomposition. The second problem is the claim. The docstring, README and guides call `mimic` output "privacy-safe synthetic twins" ([README.md](../README.md); [profiler.py](../misata/profiler.py)). The 2025 "DCR Delusion" study shows that datasets judged private by distance metrics are "highly vulnerable" to membership inference across BayNet, CTGAN and diffusion models ([arXiv 2505.01524](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.01524)). Synth-MIA finds that higher synthetic quality means more leakage ([arXiv 2509.18014](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.18014)). A non-DP fit that preserves rare categories and correlations can leak, so the claim should be replaced in 1.0 with "not privacy-protecting; may reproduce rare records".

The right architecture is **"rules first, learned residual"**. The schema decides tables, keys, cardinalities, formula columns and hard constraints. The learned model covers only the free columns, discretised on **schema-declared domains, never data-derived bounds**. Data-derived bounds leak information and silently break differential privacy, and SmartNoise documents that discretisation must not "reveal information about the distribution of the data" ([SmartNoise](https://github.com/opendp/smartnoise-sdk/blob/main/synth/README.md)). Rule-fixed columns are passed as evidence at sampling time. Remaining constraints are enforced by transform where possible, as in SDV, which tries a reversible transform first and falls back to rejection ([SDV constraints](https://sdv.dev/SDV/developer_guides/sdv/constraints.html)), and otherwise by capped rejection with a reported rejection rate. Derived columns are recomputed last, so they always hold.

For a privacy guarantee, every independent benchmark points the same way. Marginal-based select-measure-generate methods beat DP-GANs and other deep models. The NIST 2018 winner became MST ([McKenna et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.04978)). AIM "outperformed MST convincingly" and gives per-query error bounds ([AIM](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.12677)). At ε=1, AIM and PrivMRF lead ([arXiv 2504.14061](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.14061)). At ε>5, AIM-trained models reached **AUC 0.683 against 0.684 on real data** ([PLOS ONE](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10843030/)). The embeddable route is a `misata[dp]` extra on **Private-PGM (`mbi` 2.0, Apache-2.0)** with in-house or vendored MIT AIM/MST selection. SmartNoise itself pins `mbi<2` and `opacus<0.15`, which drags an old torch chain along ([PyPI smartnoise-synth](https://pypi.org/project/smartnoise-synth/)). JAX makes `mbi` heavy, so it must stay optional.

Two further modes fit Misata's no-data identity better than DP does. **Calibrate-from-aggregates** treats published cross-tabs, such as a census table of age by region, as zero-noise measurements in Private-PGM ([Private-PGM](https://github.com/ryan112358/private-pgm)). Alternatively it rakes Misata's rule-generated rows to them with IPF, the "most widely used and mature deterministic method" for population synthesis ([JASSS](https://www.jasss.org/18/2/21.html)), followed by TRS integerisation ([arXiv 1303.5228](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1303.5228)). Raking keeps every rule intact, and when the aggregates are themselves DP releases, the guarantee carries through by post-processing. **DP relational-lite** follows from a gap in the field. DP multi-table synthesis remains research-grade: PrivLava ([arXiv 2304.04545](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04545)) and Alimohammadi et al. ([arXiv 2405.18670](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.18670)) exist, but no packaged library does. So Misata should keep its own FK engine and learn only noisy fan-out histograms, clipped per root entity in the spirit of R2T, plus noisy parent-child 2-way marginals, with budget accounting per root entity. A single customer contributes many child rows, so per-row sensitivity is wrong for child tables without clipping. This is the one place where Misata could offer something no open tool packages today. It should still ship after the core, because DP code is notoriously easy to get wrong: reproductions of PATE-GAN found privacy violations ([arXiv 2406.13985](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.13985)).

## Counter-based blocks give byte-identical output across versions, machines and worker counts

Today determinism holds only within a version, and the RNG stream changed in 0.8.1.29 and 0.8.2 ([LIMITATIONS.md](../LIMITATIONS.md)). STABILITY.md promises byte-identical rows only within a MINOR series from 1.0 ([STABILITY.md](../STABILITY.md)). That is better than Faker, which guarantees nothing across patch versions ([Faker README](https://github.com/joke2k/faker/blob/master/README.rst)), and better than Hypothesis, whose replay blobs are version-locked ([Hypothesis](https://hypothesis.readthedocs.io/en/latest/reproducing.html)). It is still weaker than what the primitives allow. NumPy guarantees `Generator` streams only on "the same build of numpy, in the same environment, on the same machine", and may change them in feature releases ([NumPy compatibility](https://github.com/numpy/numpy/blob/main/doc/source/reference/random/compatibility.rst)). NEP 19, however, binds BitGenerators to stable `integers()`, `random()` and `.bytes()` streams ([NEP 19](https://github.com/numpy/numpy/blob/main/doc/neps/nep-0019-rng-policy.rst)). Philox's docstring states that a fixed seed "will always produce the same random integer stream" ([_philox.pyx](https://github.com/numpy/numpy/blob/main/numpy/random/_philox.pyx)). Rust tpchgen shows the end state is achievable. It uses integer arithmetic, and CI checks that its single- and multi-part output is byte-identical to the reference ([tpcgen-rs](https://github.com/datafusion-contrib/tpcgen-rs/blob/main/ARCHITECTURE.md)).

The design follows directly. Each value is a pure function of (seed, table, column, row), in the style of dbldatagen, where every column is "generated through some transformation of the `id` column" ([dbldatagen](https://github.com/databrickslabs/dbldatagen/blob/master/docs/source/repeatable_data_generation.rst)):

- **Keying.** Key a Philox generator from `SeedSequence([table_id, column_id, root_seed])`, with varying IDs placed before the root seed as NumPy's docs instruct ([NumPy parallel](https://github.com/numpy/numpy/blob/main/doc/source/reference/random/parallel.rst)). Column IDs come from a stable blake2b hash of the name, never Python `hash()`, which is randomised per process ([CPython](https://github.com/python/cpython/blob/main/Doc/using/cmdline.rst)).
- **Blocks.** Generate fixed-size row blocks of about 2²⁰ rows, each with its own counter offset. Rejection-based samplers consume a variable number of words, so a block-level offset is simpler than a per-row jump. A local probe confirmed that `advance()` reproduces an exact slice of the full stream. Blocks also make output independent of worker count.
- **Transforms.** Draw only raw uint64 values or uniforms, and implement every distribution in Misata: inverse-CDF normal, lognormal, Zipf, Poisson, and weighted choice by integer cumulative weights and bisection. Round money to integer cents and timestamps to integer epochs early, and avoid LAPACK-backed sampling such as `multivariate_normal`.
- **Stamp and test.** Write `misata_output_version` and the RNG algorithm into Parquet metadata and the manifest. Keep `compat="1"` selectable forever, the way NumPy froze `RandomState`. Run golden SHA-256 hashes per (schema, seed, version) in CI on Linux, macOS, Windows and ARM.
- **Exclusions.** Faker-backed and local-LLM columns sit outside the guarantee and must be labelled as such.

Philox costs little. In the local probe it produced **115M doubles/s per core**, against 314M/s for SFC64, while string assembly ran at about 1.7M names/s with `np.char` and Faker `name()` at about **8.2K/s**. The RNG is not the bottleneck.

The same block structure removes the scale limits. Fan-out becomes per-parent-block counts with prefix sums, so a child shard needs only the totals of the parent blocks it spans. Popularity-weighted FKs use a two-level inverse CDF, and a local probe ran Zipf FK assignment at about **6.0M child rows/s**. Popular parents are then spread across IDs with a keyed Feistel permutation with cycle-walking, which gives unique, random-looking, invertible keys in O(1) memory ([permuteseq](https://github.com/dverite/permuteseq)). Global `rng.choice(replace=False)` and `np.unique` deduplication must go, because they are O(N) and depend on how work is split into shards. **Roll-up tables, today held in memory, become per-block partial aggregates** (count, sum, sum of squares, min, max, a HyperLogLog sketch) merged in a final reduce, because those statistics are associative. Time-keyed blocks with entity state defined as a pure function of (entity, day) make `generate(days 0..N) + append(day N+1)` byte-identical to `generate(days 0..N+1)`. Output should stream as Arrow RecordBatches into Parquet row groups at or near PyArrow's 1,048,576-row default ([pyarrow](https://github.com/apache/arrow/blob/main/python/pyarrow/parquet/core.py)), and into Postgres through COPY, which `db.py` already uses. Distributed runs map block indices through Spark `mapInArrow`, Ray or multiprocessing. Spark's `rand()` must never be used, because its docstring calls it "non-deterministic in general case" ([pyspark](https://github.com/apache/spark/blob/master/python/pyspark/sql/functions/builtin.py)). For calibration, tpchgen writes TPC-H SF=100 in **72 s (1.4 GB/s) on a MacBook Air M3**, against about 30 minutes for classic dbgen ([DataFusion blog](https://github.com/apache/datafusion-site/blob/main/content/blog/2025-04-10-fastest-tpch-generator.md)). That sets the ceiling Misata's vectorised path should be measured against, not matched.

## Integrations are mostly built; adoption now depends on trust and distribution

The usual adoption checklist is largely done. Misata 0.9.7 already ships most of the integrations the evidence ranks highest:

- a registered `pytest11` plugin ([pyproject.toml](../pyproject.toml));
- Django and Prisma import ([prisma_import.py](../misata/prisma_import.py));
- manifest-driven dbt unit-test fixtures ([dbt_unit.py](../misata/dbt_unit.py));
- live database introspection ([introspect.py](../misata/introspect.py));
- COPY-based atomic seeding;
- Polars and Spark paths, a CLI and an MCP server.

Those integrations are how the defaults won: Faker through its pytest fixture and factory_boy, factory_boy through ORMs, Prisma users through a seed hook ([factory_boy](https://github.com/FactoryBoy/factory_boy); [Drizzle Seed](https://orm.drizzle.team/docs/seed-overview)). The remaining gap is not features. Faker sits at about **63.5M downloads a month** (a search-snippet figure, unverified) ([pypistats](https://pypistats.org/packages/faker)), and the earlier audit found Misata with a single contributor and no externally filed issues. "Describe your data and an agent seeds it" has become a commodity pitch. Tonic's Fabricate Data Agent, Seedfast and SeedBase all offer MCP seeding ([Tonic](https://www.tonic.ai/press-releases/tonic-launches-fabricate-data-agent); [Seedfast](https://seedfa.st/)). So MCP is table stakes. What differentiates is local, deterministic, verifiable output.

Four adoption levers follow from the evidence.

1. **Compose with Faker rather than replace it.** A Faker provider bridge and factory_boy and polyfactory adapters let Misata ride the incumbent's distribution.
2. **Target the orphans explicitly.** Snaplet users had to "migrate again" after Neosync's archive ([Seedfast](https://seedfa.st/blog/neosync-alternative), a competitor's source). Migration guides titled "coming from Snaplet Seed / Neosync / Synth", with a Supabase-friendly SQL output, meet them where they are.
3. **Fix the DX table stakes.** Faker's most-reacted issues are tab completion, log spam and missing type hints ([Faker issues](https://github.com/joke2k/faker/issues?q=is%3Aissue+sort%3Areactions-%2B1-desc)), and Misata itself admits about 500 unannotated internals ([LIMITATIONS.md](../LIMITATIONS.md)).
4. **Make governance visible.** Publish "MIT forever" and a semver policy, and recruit a second maintainer. Vendor-backed alternatives keep disappearing, so a credible commitment to stay open is a feature.

## The roadmap: 1.0 ships the proofs, 1.x ships the modes

The ordering principle is that **1.0 is the only release that may break the RNG stream once more**, so everything that changes how rows are generated lands there. 1.0 also ships the evidence that makes the headline claim credible. Fitting modes and extended scale arrive afterwards as additive extras that cannot destabilise the core. Effort figures are judgment calls for a single maintainer, not measurements.

| Release | Feature | Why it is high-leverage | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1.0** | **Output engine v1**: Philox per (table, column, block); Misata-owned transforms; integer money and time; `output_version` stamp; `compat=`; golden-hash CI on 4 OS/arch targets; stable sorts; no `hash()` | Converts "per-version determinism" into a guarantee no competitor offers; the last chance to break the stream | 3–4 weeks |
| **1.0** | **Text engine v2**: skeleton + slot-conditioned n-gram infill; build-time pipeline from FineWeb/Synthea plus open-weight phrase fragments; frozen bank versions; all 8 lagging kinds gated in CI | Removes the most visible remaining tell | 3–4 weeks |
| **1.0** | **Benchmark v1 public harness**: five tracks, real-vs-real ceiling, SDV/ClavaDDPM/Faker baselines, two new datasets never used for priors, JSON + HTML artefacts | Makes every other claim falsifiable | 3 weeks |
| **1.0** | **Licence and claim hygiene**: in-house copula replaces SDV in `mimic`/`[advanced]`; remove "privacy-safe"; re-derive the e-commerce prior from Online Retail II or BLS CE; NOTICE + `misata.data.licenses()` | Cheap; prevents a credibility-ending critique | 1 week |
| **1.0** | **Schema streaming with block roll-ups**: `generate_stream(schema)`, per-block partial aggregates, bounded memory | Lifts the in-memory roll-up limit and opens the path to scale | 2 weeks |
| 1.1 | **Priors pack v1**: wages (ILOSTAT/World Bank/ONS/BLS), payment mix (ECB/Fed), names (SSA/ONS/IBGE/Wikidata), GeoNames, firm sizes, for about 10 regions, all with provenance | Fixes US-flavoured economics with no legal risk | 3–4 weeks |
| 1.1 | **Calibrate from published aggregates** (IPF raking + TRS; optional `mbi` zero-noise fit) | No real rows needed, which fits Misata's identity | 2 weeks |
| 1.1 | Adoption: Faker/factory_boy bridges, migration guides, full typing pass | Distribution through incumbents | 2–3 weeks |
| 1.2 | **`misata[dp]`**: AIM default, MST fallback, explicit ε (warn above 10), schema-declared domains only, per-query error bounds in the report, optional MIA audit (reported as AUC, not DCR) | Credible privacy where it is genuinely needed | 4–6 weeks |
| 1.2 | **DP relational-lite**: fan-out clipping, noisy fan-out histograms and parent-child marginals, per-root-entity accounting | Fills a hole no packaged library covers | 4 weeks |
| 1.3+ | Distributed sharding (Spark `mapInArrow`, Ray), Feistel PKs, time-window append, opt-in local-LLM text extra, maintainer-run leaderboard, pre-registered blinded human study | Scale and community trust once the core is stable | ongoing |

The success metrics should be published with the release, so the project can be held to them:

| Area | Metric | 1.0 target |
|---|---|---|
| Determinism | Golden hashes identical across Linux/macOS/Windows/ARM and across every 1.0.x patch; block output identical for 1, 4 and 16 workers | 100% of the golden suite |
| Text | gzip ratio at 2,000 rows for all text kinds; distinct-3 | All kinds under 4.0x and distinct-3 ≥0.40; the long-form kinds under 3.5x as a stretch goal; clinical and instruction kinds labelled "no real reference" |
| Validity | Orphans, PK collisions and constraint violations on the hard-schema suite | 0 |
| Fidelity | Benchmark detection AUC (schema-only track) on new, never-tuned datasets | Published with CIs; at or below SDV fitted on the data on at least half of the datasets, with misses reported |
| Scale | Rows/s per core for numeric columns; peak memory independent of row count in streaming | Published per column type; memory flat from 10M to 1B rows |
| Adoption | Verified monthly downloads, external issues, external contributors | First independent dependents and a second maintainer |

The list of things Misata must not claim is as important as the feature list:

| Do not claim | Why | Say instead |
|---|---|---|
| "Privacy-safe" or "anonymous" twins from `mimic` | Non-DP fits can leak, and DCR-style checks give false assurance ([arXiv 2505.01524](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.01524)) | "Not privacy-protecting"; DP only with stated ε, δ, mechanism and privacy unit |
| "Indistinguishable from real data" | No method, learned or not, passes robust detection ([arXiv 2410.03411](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03411)) | "Detection AUC X on benchmark Y, real-vs-real 0.50" |
| Fidelity parity with TabDiff/ClavaDDPM on a given dataset | They train on it; Misata does not | "Within X of fitted models, with zero data access" |
| "Same bytes everywhere" for every column | Faker-backed, LLM and float-transcendental paths differ by build and machine | The guarantee scoped to Misata-owned generators and `output_version` |
| Text "a classifier can't detect" | gzip passing does not defeat n-gram classifiers | "Passes reference-free diversity and row-agreement checks" |
| Globally realistic economics | Priors cover only the regions with open data; behavioural rates are assumptions | Per-region coverage table, with assumption-labelled priors |
| "Blind" results on data used to tune priors | The Olist amount prior came from Olist | Separate tuned-on and never-seen datasets in the benchmark |
| ε ≥ 10 as "private" | Published utility parity sits at ε 5–10, where guarantees are weak ([PLOS ONE](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10843030/)) | An explicit ε with a warning |

## Conclusion

The research changes what "definitive" should mean for Misata. It is not "most realistic", because learned models trained on the target data will always win that contest on its own terms, and claiming otherwise would hand critics an easy target. It is **"most trustworthy without data"**: the only library whose output is valid by construction on any FK graph, reproducible byte-for-byte across versions and machines, honest about its text and its privacy, and measured in public against the tools that did see the data. Each of those properties is a contract rather than a score, and contracts are what CI pipelines, fixture files and agents can depend on. Contracts are also what made Faker and factory_boy defaults.

Two non-obvious implications follow. First, 1.0 should be judged by what it freezes, not what it adds. The determinism engine and text engine v2 both change bytes, so shipping them before the semver promise begins is what makes the promise affordable afterwards. Second, the largest remaining risk is not technical. A single-maintainer project making strong guarantees needs outside verification: independent benchmark reruns, a second maintainer and a first wave of external issues. That evidence is what will show whether the guarantees are being trusted. The uncertainties that remain open are worth stating. The clinical and instruction text thresholds have no licence-clean real reference to calibrate against. Several licence readings came from snippets and need primary-page checks. No published measurement yet shows how much rule enforcement degrades DP fidelity.
